## How to read this document

Each claim used on the site is listed with the figure, the source, and a **credibility tier**:

- **Tier 1 — Primary / authoritative:** peer-reviewed journals, government transport-authority reports, published material standards (ASTM/ISO/ACI).
- **Tier 2 — Supporting:** industry/manufacturer technical pages and trade press, used only to corroborate Tier 1 ranges (never as the sole basis for a claim).
- **Tier 3 — Context only:** promotional video / AI summaries supplied as background. **Not** used as the basis for any numeric claim.

> Note on downloads: open-access papers are marked **[Open access]** and can be downloaded directly from the link. Paywalled papers are marked **[Abstract only]** — the abstract and headline figures are public; the full PDF needs journal/institutional access. Direct PDF saving wasn't possible from this environment, so links are provided for one-click download.

## 5. BasaltechSA factory tensile test (supplied by client)

Source: BasaltechSA factory test video of the finished mesh (on file). Reported by the client:

| Result | Value |
|---|---|
| Single strand | **5.571 kN** |
| Machine direction (per 1 m width) | 19.68 strands × 5.571 kN = **109.64 kN/m** |
| Cross (transverse) direction | up to **120 kN/m** |
| Manufacturing note | Made to a higher standard for export orders, with a safety margin to allow for differences in overseas test methods |
| Test note | Slippage can occur with winding-type fixtures if not properly tightened, which can lower recorded readings |

**Context (for credibility framing):** ~110–120 kN/m places BasaltechSA mesh at the **upper end of, or above,** the 50–100 kN/m typical of commercial basalt geogrids (see §4) and well above the 60 kN/m grid used in the cited asphalt study. The strand spacing implied (1000 mm ÷ 19.68 ≈ 50.8 mm) is a standard ~50 mm aperture.

## 8. Notes / cautions on existing website claims

Flagged so the site stays defensible in front of engineers:

1. **"Increases flexural strength by up to 55%"** and **"reduces shrinkage microcracks by up to 90%"** (current Fibre section) are higher than the typical peer-reviewed range I found (flexural gains often ~10–25% at common dosages). These may come from a specific high-dosage study or supplier marketing. Recommend either sourcing them to a specific reference or softening to research-backed ranges.
2. **"Three times stronger than steel"** (Fibre section) vs **"2.5× tensile strength vs steel"** (hero/properties) are inconsistent. Cleanest framing: **BFRP rebar ≈ 2–2.5× steel rebar**; **raw basalt fibre** is several times stronger again at the filament level. Keep the two comparisons clearly separate.
3. **Comparison table** lists basalt fibre tensile as **1,100–1,400 MPa** — that's the BFRP composite/bar range, not raw continuous fibre (3,000–4,840 MPa). Fine to keep if labelled as the composite/bar value, but worth a footnote so a specifier isn't confused.
